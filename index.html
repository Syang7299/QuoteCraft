How this contractor estimate calculator works

Most estimate templates only ask for a lump sum, which makes it easy to accidentally underprice a job. This calculator builds your quote in layers instead, so you can see exactly where the money goes before you send it to a client.

Labor, materials, and subcontractor costs are added together as your direct costs. Overhead is added as a flat amount on top. Contingency and markup are then applied as percentages on the running subtotal. Profit margin is calculated last using the true margin formula, so it represents a share of your final quote rather than another cost-plus layer - a detail most free calculators get wrong, and one that can quietly cost you real money on every job.

Estimate terms, explained

Labor

The cost of your own time and any employees working the job, usually hours 脳 rate.

Materials

Everything you're buying for the job itself - lumber, fixtures, paint, hardware, and so on.

Subcontractor

Any portion of the job you're paying another contractor or specialist to handle.

Overhead

The fixed cost of running your business day to day - insurance, vehicle, tools, software - allocated to this job.

Contingency

A buffer percentage to cover surprises once the job is underway, without eating into your profit.

Markup vs. profit margin

Markup adds a percentage on top of your cost. Profit margin is the percentage of your final price that's actual profit - getting it right means dividing, not adding, which is why a 15% margin and a 15% markup produce different totals.
