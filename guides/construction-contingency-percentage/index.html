Construction Contingency: How Much Should You Budget?

Contingency isn't padding. It's the honest acknowledgment that some things - what's actually behind that wall, what steel costs in month four of the job - won't be known until you're already underway.

QuoteCraft Guides · Updated September 2026

Skip contingency and you're betting the entire job goes exactly as planned. Price it right and a surprise mid-project becomes a manageable line item instead of a loss.

What contingency is actually for

Contingency covers genuine unknowns: conditions hidden until demolition starts, material price swings over a long job, or design details that firm up after the estimate is signed. It is not a place to hide underbid scope or pad the number because a client seems price-sensitive - those are separate problems, and folding them into contingency just hides them until they surface as a loss.

Typical contingency percentages by project type

The right number depends heavily on how much is actually unknown at the time you're pricing the job:

Project typeTypical contingency
New construction, complete design3-5%
New construction, early/schematic design5-10%
Renovation / adaptive reuse10-15%
Historic preservation / older buildings15-20%+

As a general default when you don't have a more specific number, most commercial construction projects land in the 5-10% range of hard construction costs.

What actually drives the number

Rather than picking a percentage off a chart, three factors should drive your actual figure:

  • Design completeness - a fully detailed design leaves less room for surprises than an early schematic one, so it needs less contingency
  • Site and building age - the older the building or the less known about site conditions, the more likely you'll find something once work starts (outdated wiring, water damage, non-standard framing)
  • Material and schedule volatility - a longer project timeline means more exposure to price swings on materials that haven't been purchased yet

A new-construction job on a well-documented site with a complete design set can justify a contingency near the low end of the range. A renovation of a 1920s building with an incomplete design should sit near the high end - or above it.

Calculating the dollar amount

Contingency allocation

Contingency $ = (Direct Costs + Overhead) × Contingency %

Worked example

Direct costs + overhead subtotal: $18,000

Contingency for renovation-level risk: 10%

Contingency to add: $18,000 × 0.10 = $1,800

FAQ

What is a typical contingency percentage in construction?

Most commercial construction projects use 5-10% of hard construction costs. New construction with a complete design can run as low as 3-5%, while renovations of older buildings commonly need 10-15%, and historic preservation work can run 15-20% or higher.

Why do renovations need a higher contingency than new construction?

New construction starts from known, empty ground with a complete design. Renovation work uncovers conditions that can't be seen until walls, floors, or ceilings are opened - outdated wiring, water damage, non-standard framing - so it carries meaningfully more risk of the unknown.

How do I calculate the contingency dollar amount for an estimate?

Contingency $ = (Direct Costs + Overhead) × Contingency %. For example, a job with an $18,000 subtotal and a 10% contingency for renovation-level risk carries $1,800 in contingency.
