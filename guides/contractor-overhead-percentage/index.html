How Much Overhead Should a Contractor Charge?

Overhead is the cost of staying in business - not the job in front of you. Leave it out of a quote and every job you do ends up quietly subsidizing your rent, insurance, and office phone bill.

QuoteCraft Guides · Updated September 2026

If your estimates only account for materials, labor, and subs, you're pricing the job - but not the business behind it. Overhead is what keeps the lights on between jobs, and it needs its own line.

What actually counts as overhead

The simplest test: can you bill this expense to one specific job? If not, it's overhead.

  • Overhead (not job-specific): office rent, insurance premiums, admin/office staff salaries, software subscriptions, equipment loan payments, accounting fees, phone and utilities, vehicle maintenance
  • Direct costs (billed to the job): materials, job-site labor, subcontractors, permits

The overhead percentage formula

Overhead rate

Overhead % = (Total Annual Overhead ÷ Total Annual Revenue) × 100

Worked example

Annual overhead: $150,000

Annual revenue: $500,000

Overhead rate: $150,000 ÷ $500,000 × 100 = 30%

Some contractors calculate the same ratio against total direct costs instead of revenue, which produces a different-looking percentage for the exact same business. Neither method is wrong, but they aren't interchangeable - always check which base a published benchmark is using before comparing it to your own number.

Turning your rate into a dollar figure for one job

An annual percentage is useful for planning, but an estimate needs a dollar amount. QuoteCraft's Overhead field takes a flat dollar figure for the job, so convert your rate like this:

Job overhead allocation

Job Overhead $ = Direct Job Costs × Overhead %

Worked example

Direct costs for this job (labor + materials + subcontractor + permits): $25,000

Your overhead rate: 30%

Overhead to allocate to this job: $25,000 × 0.30 = $7,500

Once you have that dollar figure, drop it into QuoteCraft's free calculator alongside labor, materials, and your target margin for a full recommended quote.

FAQ

How do you calculate contractor overhead percentage?

Overhead % = (Total Annual Overhead ÷ Total Annual Revenue) × 100. For example, $150,000 in annual overhead against $500,000 in annual revenue works out to a 30% overhead rate. Some contractors calculate it against total direct costs instead of revenue, which produces a different-looking number for the same business - always check which base a benchmark uses before comparing it to your own.

What is a typical overhead percentage for a contractor?

As a percentage of revenue, overhead commonly runs 25-50% depending on trade - roughly 25-38% for painting, 28-42% for electrical, 30-45% for plumbing and roofing, and 35-50% for HVAC, remodeling, and general contracting.

How do I turn my overhead rate into a dollar amount for one job?

Multiply that job's direct costs (labor, materials, subcontractor, permits) by your overhead percentage: Job Overhead $ = Direct Job Costs × Overhead %. A job with $25,000 in direct costs and a 30% overhead rate carries about $7,500 in overhead - that's the dollar figure to enter into an estimate's Overhead line.
